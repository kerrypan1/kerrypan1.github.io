CS 180 · Project 1

Images of the Russian Empire

Colorizing the Prokudin-Gorskii photo collection

Overview

Each input is a glass plate with three exposures stacked from top to bottom in BGR order. I split the image into three equal parts, align green and red to blue with (x, y) translations, and stack the channels into an RGB image. Images are converted to floats first, and I apply shifts with np.roll.

I tried both MSE and NCC. MSE looked better on the small jpgs, so I kept it for single-scale alignment; the pyramid results use NCC. The green and red displacements are listed under each image.

Single-Scale Alignment (L2 / MSE)

For the low-res jpgs I search every displacement in [-15, 15], rolling the channel at each (x, y) and scoring how well it matches blue.

I used MSE, the mean of (image1 − image2)2, which is proportional to the L2 / Euclidean distance in the spec, sqrt(sum(sum((image1-image2).^2))). MSE just drops the square root and divides by the number of pixels, so the displacement that minimizes MSE is the same one that minimizes L2. I also tried NCC on these images, but MSE looked better, so I kept it.

Scores are computed on interior pixels only. I crop by the window size because np.roll wraps pixels around the edges, which would otherwise throw off the error. That was enough to align cathedral, monastery, and tobolsk.

Multi-Scale Pyramid (NCC)

On the full-size tifs the true offset is often much larger than 15 pixels, and searching a wide window at full resolution is too slow, so I used an image pyramid: run the search on a smaller version first, then refine as the image is brought back up to full size.

Unlike single-scale, the pyramid uses NCC. After cropping, I mean-subtract each channel, normalize, and take their dot product (higher is a better match). That generally aligned the large images better than L2, which is why I switched.

I downsample by 2 with anti-aliasing until both dimensions are under 256, search [-15, 15] at that coarsest scale, then recurse back up. At each finer level I take twice the coarse offset and search only ±2 around it, still ignoring borders. The offsets below are at full resolution.

Images of My Choosing

I ran the same NCC pyramid on three additional plates from the Prokudin-Gorskii collection.

Failures and Lessons

NCC helped on most of the large images, but it made Emir much worse (red offset (−205, 141)). The robe is very bright in blue and dark in red, so even after mean-subtraction the two channels still don't look alike. The assignment flags this case; aligning on edges or gradients instead of brightness would probably help.
